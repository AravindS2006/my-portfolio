01 / THE CONTEXT

Exploring accessible ocular screening.

AirTon is a student-team effort exploring a portable ocular-screening prototype. The project brings sensor hardware and machine-learning exploration together around glaucoma screening.

02 / MY CONTRIBUTION

Technical lead. ML contributor.

I am the technical lead of the project and worked on its machine-learning component using AI tools. That is the contribution I can stand behind; I do not present the work as independently developed clinical AI.

The project gave me a reason to connect an engineering idea to a real-world question.

03 / WORK & EVIDENCE

A prototype with a wider research context.

The project materials describe ESP32-based hardware, sensors and Python processing. The associated institutional project, “Retino-Oculometric Dual-Layer System for Rapid Ocular Health Triage,” is listed by EPICS in IEEE with $5,420 in team-project funding.

The official record establishes that project’s title, institution and funding. It does not establish an individual cash award or a clinical result. The precise relationship between versions of the AirTon prototype and the funded project is still being documented.

Read the IEEE project recordAssociated web application source

Current scope: a student research prototype. No clinical validation, regulatory approval, patient outcomes or model accuracy is claimed here.

04 / WHAT I LEARNED

Make the contribution clear.

Technical leadership and contributing to an ML component are parts of a larger team effort. For the next stage, documenting the prototype versions, test methods and individual contributions will make the work easier to evaluate and build on.
